State: WORKS, WITH FULL-SCREEN CONTROL. First practice run on 5 Nov 2026 (Gemini app 1.138.0.1315). Claude opened the app, started a new chat, chose "Create images", sent the test prompt and had the picture in about 12 seconds. It then got the picture out two ways and looked at each filed file: Way B (copy the picture, then `clip_image`) gave a 1024×1024 PNG, and Way A (its download icon, a Save panel, `wait_new`, `ingest`) gave a 1024×1024 JPEG. Both are the right picture (one white triangle on flat magenta, no text, no mark). Way B is the better one. A second test attached a reference picture ("+" > Files, then a typed path in the file picker) and asked for the same picture with a green triangle: the answer was an exact edit of the reference, so references work. A third test chose 9:16 in the aspect menu: the picture came back as a 768×1376 PNG (a hair narrower than exact 9:16), and a flat "pure green (#00FF00)" background came out as about RGB (18, 249, 7) with ±15 levels of noise, not exactly #00FF00. The picture's own buttons (a hover toolbar) and its right-click menu (Copy Image, Save Image…) need a real mouse, so saving, copying and attaching need full-screen control, with the owner at the Mac and no other app's window on top of Gemini (a ChatGPT window made every click fail once). The background tools alone cannot do it. The game's cut-out tool (its new edge) cut Gemini's flat green and magenta pictures cleanly, checked without installing anything. A real-sprite test (Nanami, with a copy of the game's own sprite as the reference, one change: a smile, 9:16, on magenta) worked: Gemini kept the character, outfit, pose and scale and gave a warm closed-mouth smile on a flat magenta backdrop with no mark, filed as a 768×1376 PNG. It had to be copied out by the owner's own hand (right-click the picture, Copy Image), because my clicks stopped working while the full-screen overlay was on. The game's cut-out tool, run on it without installing anything, was NOT clean with its new edge on this real figure (a faint lilac-pink rim and a few faint specks); the old edge looked cleaner. See "A real sprite through Gemini". Not tried yet: the system-wide shortcuts. The details are in "UI notes".

The Gemini Mac app has no scripting door. The only way to use it is screen control: something has to look at the window, then click and type in it. desk-art does NOT include a mouse or keyboard driver. Instead, Claude uses its own built-in Computer use tools, when they exist in the session. desk-art only supplies the helpers around that: `wait_new`, `clip_image` and `ingest`. The loop and the prompt recipe are in `../SKILL.md`.

Every picture uses your own Google plan allowance, which is limited and which desk-art cannot see. Do not batch runs.

## Why screen control

Checked on this Mac (the first three) or read (the last one), on 4 Nov 2026:

- The app is `com.google.GeminiMacOS`, version 1.138. It is a normal Mac app, not sandboxed. Its own information file says it needs macOS 15 or later.
- It has no AppleScript commands.
- Its Shortcuts actions are only Ask Gemini, Open Conversation and Conversations Needing Attention. None of them makes a picture.
- Google's Gemini command-line program stopped serving individual users on 18 Jun 2026.

How Claude's Computer use treats apps (from Anthropic's docs, read on 4 Nov 2026): browsers are view-only. Terminals and IDEs are click-only, with no typing. Everything else gets full control. So this route uses the Mac app, never the Gemini website. Antigravity is an IDE, which is one reason it goes through its command-line program instead.

## Steps for you (once)

What it needs: macOS 14 or later, and a Claude Pro, Max or Ultra plan. Team and Enterprise plans do not have it. Anthropic calls it a research preview. The Gemini app itself needs macOS 15 or later, and it must be signed in with your Google account.

1. Update the Claude app to the newest version, then quit it and open it again.
2. Open Settings in the Claude app, then "This computer", then "System". If Settings has no "This computer", look under "Capabilities".
3. Under "Computer use", turn on "Enable computer use".
4. macOS then needs two permissions for the Claude app: Accessibility and Screen Recording. Accessibility lets the Claude app click and type for you. Screen Recording lets it see your screen. The Settings page shows whether each one is allowed. When one is denied, it opens System Settings for you. Turn the Claude app on there, for each one. You can turn both off again later in System Settings, under Privacy & Security.
5. Close or hide anything private on your screen. Computer use works from screenshots of the screen, which go to Claude.
6. Start a new Claude session. This is needed: on 5 Nov 2026 a session that was already open when Computer use was turned on had no Computer use tools, and a new session had them.
7. Ask Claude for a picture from the Gemini app. Claude asks to use the Gemini app. Choose "Allow for this session" for the Gemini app only. The other choice is "Deny".
8. To get the finished picture out, Claude needs full-screen control for a minute or two. Be at the Mac. It takes over the mouse and keyboard, and it hides your other apps' windows (they come back from the Dock). Close anything that floats over other windows first, such as a ChatGPT chat bar: while another app's window is on top of Gemini, Claude is not allowed to click.

## Playbook for Claude

Use this only when the Computer use tools exist in the session (in the desktop app their names start with `mcp__computer-use__`). If a skill named `computer-use` is listed, read it first.

If the tools do not exist, give the owner "Steps for you (once)" above and stop. Do not make your own scripts that click or type (AppleScript, key-posting tricks or any other home-made clicker). The only use of `osascript` in desk-art is inside `clip_image`, and it only reads the clipboard picture.

1. **Ask for access to the Gemini app only.** Not a browser, not Finder, not Terminal. The owner approves each app in the session prompt. Full-screen control (step 8) is not another app. If a click is refused because some other app's window is on top, do not add that app: see "Rules".
2. **Look.** Take a screenshot. Is the app open? Is it signed in? Is there a dialog? If the Computer use tools can read the window as text, as well as a picture, read the text first. It is more reliable for finding buttons.
3. **Start a new chat in the full window.** With the background tools, use the app's menu bar (`app_menu`): File > Open app opens the window when the app has none, and File > New chat gives a fresh chat (checked 5 Nov 2026). The system-wide shortcuts, Option+Shift+Space for the full window and Option+Space for a small overlay, have not been tried. One picture per chat keeps things clean.
4. **Find image generation.** Press the "+" button at the left end of the prompt row (the window text calls it "Attach") and choose "Create images". It uses Nano Banana (checked 5 Nov 2026).
5. **Type the prompt.** Write it the way `../SKILL.md` says (the prompt recipe and the scope rule apply here too).
6. **Attach a reference, if there is one.** A picture attached in the Gemini app is uploaded to Google. desk-art cannot check this step, because the tools do not see the screen. So follow the same rule yourself: attach only a picture from the folders desk-art may send from (see `../SKILL.md`, "Which folders pictures may come from"), and tell the owner first. Name the reference in the `note` when you file the result. What worked on 5 Nov 2026: choose "Create images" first, then press "+" again and choose "Files". Never choose "Select windows" or "Select from screen" (they share the screen). A macOS file picker opens, usually on the owner's Downloads folder: do not look through it. Switch to full-screen control (`app_release`, `request_full_control`, `open_application`), press Cmd+Shift+G, type the full path of the file (the old text in the box is selected, so typing replaces it), press Return (the picker jumps to the folder with the file selected), check with a screenshot that the right file is highlighted, and press Return again. A thumbnail of the picture shows at the top of the prompt box and the send button comes alive. Dragging a file in from Finder, or pasting it, was not needed (and would need access to Finder). For an anchor set, attach the set's files in the numbered order that the anchor skill's `words` command prints, one after the other, pick the Pro or Flash model (not Flash-Lite, which Google says is not built for several reference pictures), paste the `prompt` that `words` printed (it prints one only when you run `words` with `--prompt-stdin` and give the request as the text after `<<<`; the anchor skill's by-hand steps show the exact command), and name the set in the `note` of `clip_image`. This route is outside every automatic check.
7. **Submit** and wait. Look at the window every few seconds. If nothing has appeared after about five minutes, stop and tell the owner. The send button is called "Submit" and only exists while there is text in the box. The test picture took about 12 seconds. If the reply is "Something went wrong" with a "Try again" button, do not rely on that button (pressed with the background tools it did nothing, twice, on 5 Nov 2026): start a new chat and send the prompt once more, and if that fails too, stop and tell the owner.
8. **Get the picture out.** This needs full-screen control, with the owner at the Mac, because the picture's own buttons and its right-click menu need a real mouse (and the buttons are not in the window text). Call `app_release` for the Gemini app, then `request_full_control` and `open_application`, then take a screenshot. First check that no other app's window is on top of Gemini (a ChatGPT window made every click fail once). The most reliable way: right-click the middle of the picture. A small menu opens at the pointer with "Copy Image" and "Save Image…". Click "Copy Image" (Way B). The other way: move the mouse over the picture in a few small steps and wait a second, then zoom in on the picture's top-right corner. A small toolbar shows three icons, left to right: copy, download, share. NEVER press share. (In the first chat the toolbar showed after a few small steps. In a second chat, eight tries in a row did not bring it up, and the right-click menu worked at once.) Do not just click the picture: that opens a macOS Quick Look preview (Escape closes it; never press its "Open with Preview" or its share icon). There are two ways to get the picture out. Try Way B first. When you are done, call `release_full_control`.

   Way A, save it to a file (the fallback: it works, but it needs a Save panel and gives a JPEG):
   1. Before you save, run `date +%s` with Bash and keep the number. That is the time to wait from.
   2. Press the download icon (the middle one) of the hover toolbar (the right-click menu's "Save Image…" is probably the same, but it was not tried). A Save panel opens. It starts with the name "image" and the folder Desktop. Type a new file name (the old name is already selected). Then set "Where" to Downloads: click the "Where" button, type `Downloads` to highlight that entry, and press Return (clicks on the open drop-down menu are refused). Check with a zoom that the panel says Downloads, then press the Save button.
   3. Call `wait_new` with `sinceEpoch` set to that number. Leave `dir` empty to watch Downloads, or give the folder you saved to. It waits until a new picture file appears and has stopped growing. It only looks. It files nothing.
   4. Call `ingest` with the file path from `wait_new`, a `label`, `backend` set to `gemini-app`, and the prompt you used. This files the picture into the inbox. `ingest` reads only from `~/Downloads`, the folders desk-art may send from, and the two generator picture folders (see `../SKILL.md`), so save into Downloads. The saved file is a JPEG even when it is named `.png`; `ingest` files it with the right ending.

   Way B, copy it (try this first: no Save panel, and a lossless PNG):
   1. Right-click the middle of the picture and click "Copy Image", or press the copy icon (the first one) of the hover toolbar if the toolbar shows. Nothing shows on screen when it works. This replaces whatever the owner had on the clipboard, so tell the owner afterwards.
   2. Call `clip_image` with a `label`, `backend` set to `gemini-app`, and the prompt. It reads the picture on the clipboard and files it into the inbox. It does not need `ingest`.
   3. Do not copy anything else in between. `clip_image` files whatever picture is on the clipboard at that moment.
   4. If it says `no-image-on-clipboard`, the copy did not work. Check that you pressed the icon on the picture (the "Copy response" button under the picture copies only words). Copy again and call it again. If it says `osascript-failed` or `not-an-image`, use Way A instead.
9. **Look at the picture** with the Read tool before you say anything about it. If you see a mark, logo or text on it, tell the owner. Look at a corner enlarged too: a small mark could hide there.
10. **Ask the owner** which to keep, then hand over as in `../SKILL.md`.

## The game's cut-out tool on Gemini's pictures

Checked on 5 Nov 2026 with three flat test pictures: the 9:16 one on green, and two on magenta (a white and a green triangle). Nothing was installed and nothing in the game folder changed. `keysprite.py` itself was NOT run, because it installs into `game/images/char`. Instead its own cutting and checking code was run from a scratch script that wrote only to a scratch folder (Python's bytecode cache switched off; a time marker showed that no file in the game folder changed, and git still showed the same 30 entries as before). Only the last step, the save, was skipped.

- `keyedge.py` (the new edge, `--edge new`) read Gemini's backdrops as RGB (18, 247, 9) for the "pure green" and (249, 1, 246) and (251, 2, 248) for the magenta, and cut all three cleanly. Its own `--check` found 0 strongly tinted pixels and no backdrop pocket left solid (one faint pixel on one picture). The tool's QA was clean on two pictures and had one complaint on the third: 1 edge pixel still carrying the backdrop's colour (0.001% of the figure).
- The edge is about 1 pixel wide, there are no holes, nothing is left in the clear pixels, and the figure box the tool measured matches the real triangle, so sprite scaling is not thrown off. A few dozen faint specks (alpha 72 of 255 at most) sit within 3 px of the figure: outline fringe, not dust.
- Edge colour, looked at 8 times enlarged on grey: a slight green tint on white cut from green (about +6 levels), a slight pink tint on white cut from magenta (about −9), and a thin dark purple-grey rim on green cut from magenta (seen on a light background). All faint.
- The old default edge (`--edge old`, and no option at all) also passed the tool's QA, but it was worse: 19 pinholes in the white (against none), a 3 px soft edge on the green one, and a clearly visible dark purple rim on the magenta ones (magenta tint 10.7 against 2.3, and −13.7 against −8.6). On these flat pictures `--edge new` was the better edge, as `docs/ASSET_PIPELINE.md` says for any new sprite; on the real sprite in the next section it was not.
- The exact backdrop colour does not matter: the cutter reads the backdrop at every pixel, so Gemini's slightly dull green and its rose-ish magenta are fine. A grey or white backdrop would not be.
- A real character (hair, fine lines) was tried on 6 Nov: see the next section.

## Rules

- Never click links. Not in the chat, not in a dialog.
- Do not use a browser for this route.
- If a dialog asks for a password, a code or a purchase, STOP and ask the owner. Do not type anything for them.
- If an unexpected dialog appears (an update, a sign-in, an offer, terms), stop and tell the owner what it says. Do not click through.
- Ask for access to the Gemini app only, and nothing else.
- If a click is refused because another app's window is on top of Gemini ("would land on ChatGPT, which is not in the allowed applications"), do not add that app to the access list. Stop and ask the owner to close or move that window.
- The Save panel belongs to a macOS helper, "Open and Save Panel Service (Gemini)". Do not add the helper to the access list either. Type in the panel and click its own buttons. For its open drop-down menu, use the keyboard.
- Never press the Gemini app's Share button at the top right (it is meant to share the chat; it was not opened), the third icon (share) of the picture's hover toolbar, or "Export to Docs" or "Draft in Gmail" in the picture's "More actions" menu. They send the picture or the chat to other Google services.
- Do not open the owner's other chats (the Chats menu in the menu bar and the sidebar list their titles) or the sidebar's Library. They are the owner's private things.
- Never choose "Select windows" or "Select from screen" in the "+" menu: they share your windows or your screen with Google.
- The file picker opens on the owner's own folders (it showed the Downloads folder). Do not look through them and do not open anything there: go straight to the file you need with Cmd+Shift+G and a typed path from the folders desk-art may send from.
- In the Quick Look preview that a click on the picture opens, never press "Open with Preview" or the share icon. Press Escape.
- Never type into the file picker until a screenshot shows its "Go to the folder" box open. If the keys go to the Gemini prompt box instead, Return sends the typed text as a prompt.
- Do not work around a refusal such as "belongs to a different process" or "would land on ChatGPT". Stop and tell the owner.
